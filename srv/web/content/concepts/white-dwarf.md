# White Dwarfs

A white dwarf is the compact remnant left when many stars finish ordinary fusion and shed their outer layers. Roughly stellar mass can be compressed into a body comparable in size to Earth. Electron degeneracy pressure supports the remnant against further collapse.

White dwarfs do not all look alike. Their atmospheric composition, temperature, magnetic field, mass, companions, and cooling age affect what astronomers observe. A white-dwarf classification should therefore come from applicable compact-object or spectral evidence, not merely from a low estimated luminosity.

## In a system

White dwarfs can retain planets and stellar companions. In a multiple system, the remnant records a different evolutionary history from unevolved companions and can constrain the system's age.
