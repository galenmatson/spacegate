# Brown Dwarfs

Brown dwarfs form between the conventional domains of stars and giant planets. They are not massive enough to sustain ordinary hydrogen fusion like main-sequence stars, although the more massive examples can fuse deuterium or lithium for limited periods.

The **L, T, and Y** spectral sequence follows increasingly cool atmospheres. Condensate clouds shape many L-type spectra, methane is prominent in many T dwarfs, and Y dwarfs occupy the coldest recognized brown-dwarf class. Mass alone does not uniquely determine spectral class because brown dwarfs cool as they age.

Spacegate does not call an object a rogue planet merely because no orbit is recorded. Formation history and gravitational binding require evidence of their own.
