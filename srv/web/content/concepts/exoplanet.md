# Exoplanets

An exoplanet is a planet beyond the Solar System. Transit surveys detect periodic dimming when a planet crosses its star. Radial-velocity measurements detect the star's motion around a shared center of mass. Direct imaging, microlensing, timing, and astrometry reveal other parts of the population.

Different methods constrain different quantities. A transit commonly supplies radius relative to the star and orbital period; radial velocity commonly constrains a minimum mass. Spacegate preserves the selected values and their evidence rather than pretending every planet was measured in the same way.

## Broad map categories

The six map categories combine a broad size-or-mass proxy with hot, temperate, or cold irradiation. “Jupiter” is a readable map label for gas-giant-like objects, not a claim that each resembles Jupiter in composition. “Terrestrial” describes scale, not an Earth-like surface.
